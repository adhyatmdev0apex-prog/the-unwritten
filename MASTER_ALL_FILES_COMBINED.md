## 2. Narrative Beat Progression

### ACT 1 — THE LAST BELL (0:00–0:12 | 12s)
* **0:00–0:05 — Classroom Opening:** Fade in from black. Camera at the back of the classroom, slightly elevated, sweeping down into a straight-on perspective with a slight zoom out. Students finish work in warm afternoon sunlight.
* **0:05–0:10 — The Bell:** School bell rings. Students pack bags, stand, and file out. Lucien and Elara remain at their desk.
* **0:10–0:12 — A Strange Connection:**
  * **ELARA:** *"You're not even going to pretend you were studying?"*
  * **LUCIEN:** *"I was trying to."*
  * A tiny smile. A golden crack opens in the floor shadow beneath their desk.
  * **ELARA:** *"Lucien…?"*
  * The crack spreads across the floor.

---

### ACT 2 — THEIR SCHOOL, BEYOND REALITY (0:12–0:48 | 36s) — *[40% EXPLORATION CORE]*
*THIS IS THE MAIN EVENT — The audience thinks: "I wish my school looked like that!"*

* **0:12–0:18 (6s) — The Impossible Fall:** The classroom floor opens into vast impossible space. Lucien and Elara fall past fragments of different worlds: an upside-down forest, stone arches floating in darkness, an ocean suspended above the sky. They reach for each other as they fall, then land on a black-stone platform above the clouds. Hands release.
* **0:18–0:25 (7s) — The Fantasy School Reveal:** Lucien and Elara look up. Their ordinary school has transformed into an enormous Gothic academy above the clouds: familiar classroom windows glowing teal, floating bridges connecting towering buildings, staircases rearranging in mid-air, central dome rotating slowly.
  * **ELARA:** *"Where are we?"*
  * **LUCIEN:** *"I wish I knew."*
* **0:25–0:32 (7s) — The Enchanted Classroom:** They enter a classroom unlike anything they've seen. Desks float gently in mid-air. A blackboard transforms into a moving constellation. Holographic mathematical equations orbit the room. A miniature solar system spins above a teacher's desk. One equation folds into a glowing doorway. Elara reaches toward a floating equation—it responds to her hand. Wonder, not fear.
* **0:32–0:39 (7s) — The Library Between Worlds:** They follow the glowing doorway into a monumental library. Books orbit a spiral staircase. A floating platform carries tomes between shelves. A window reveals galaxies instead of the sky. A book opens by itself, displaying an illustration of the academy with a strange symbol matching the golden crack from their classroom. Lucien notices and shows Elara. Wonder turns into mystery.
* **0:39–0:48 (9s) — The Forbidden Corridor:** They follow the symbol into a long corridor where all portraits are draped in dark cloth. The lights extinguish one by one behind them. At the far end stands a tall sealed door bearing the exact symbol. A faint sound comes from the other side.
  * **ELARA:** *"Something's in there."*
  * **LUCIEN:** *"Then why does it feel like it's waiting for us?"*
  * The sealed door opens by itself.

---

### ACT 3 — THE CHAMBER THAT REMEMBERS (0:48–1:05 | 17s)
* **0:48–0:53 (5s) — The Sphere:** They step into a circular black-basalt chamber. A perfectly dark black-glass sphere floats above a stone platform, surrounded by rotating bronze rings. Golden fissures move beneath its surface. The room becomes almost completely silent.
* **0:53–1:00 (7s) — The Marks & Silhouettes:** Lucien approaches the sphere. The golden fissures rearrange into a pattern resembling two intertwined hands. Elara reaches toward it. Matching golden marks appear on both their palms.
  * **ELARA:** *"Have we been here before?"*
  * **LUCIEN:** *"I've never seen this place."*
  * The sphere reveals silhouettes resembling them in unfamiliar worlds under foreign stars.
  * **ELARA:** *"Then why does this feel familiar?"*
  * Lucien has no answer.
* **1:00–1:05 (5s) — Something Behind the Glass:** The golden light disappears into black. Something enormous shifts in the darkness behind the sphere. The bronze rings decelerate. A low rumble echoes, like a distant heartbeat.

---

### ACT 4 — THE CHOICE & CLIMAX (1:05–1:30 | 25s)
* **1:05–1:12 (7s) — The Decision:** Elara looks toward the darkness. She reaches for Lucien's hand, hesitates, and stops. Lucien notices, looks at the sphere, then at Elara. He deliberately takes her hand.
  * **LUCIEN:** *"Whatever that thing is… we face it together."*
  * Their golden marks connect into a single interwoven symbol. The sphere flares with golden light.
* **1:12–1:21 (9s) — The Academy Awakens:** Golden energy races through the chamber walls and spreads outward across the entire academy: the enchanted classroom freezes mid-air, floating books freeze, the rotating dome grinds to a dead halt, every window turns black one by one, and clouds spiral around a vast darkness. The beautiful school has become something else.
* **1:21–1:27 (6s) — The Eye:** Back inside the chamber, the darkness behind the sphere shifts. Deep in the void, an enormous golden eye slowly opens, reflecting the tiny silhouettes of Lucien and Elara. Neither speaks. Their joined hands are the only warm light in the room.
* **1:27–1:30 (3s) — Title:** SMASH CUT TO BLACK. A thin molten-gold fissure cracks across the darkness and forms:
  # THE UNWRITTEN
  One deep cinematic impact. Silence.

## 🛡️ THE VISUAL CONTINUITY ANCHOR TECHNIQUE
1. **Never ask the model to jump-cut within a single 10-second clip.** Every clip is designed as a single continuous fluid optical movement (dolly, crane rise, tilt, rack-focus).

# World and Mythology

## The ordinary world
A believable school classroom after the last bell: wooden desks, fluorescent light, bags on chairs, corridor outside, dusk light. The more real this feels, the bigger the crossing feels.
**Continuity:** desk positions, windows, light direction and the portal location stay fixed throughout Act I.

## The portal
Not a blue or purple ring. Darkness that absorbs light. Six states:
1. Shadows stretch toward a fixed point.
2. Small objects move against gravity.
3. The darkest region loses surface detail.
4. Thin golden fissures appear inside it.
5. Fissures spread as the opening expands.
6. A physical view into another world.

## The academy (The Meridian - working name)
- **Materials:** weathered dark stone, aged bronze, blackened metal, tall carved wooden doors, teal stained glass, reflective black surfaces, warm gold magical light.
- **Motifs:** Gothic arches, impossible vertical spaces, bridges over clouds, independently moving staircases, endless libraries, astronomical chambers.
- **Light:** cool in shadow, warm gold near magic, volumetric light through windows. Reserve the biggest effects for key moments.
- **Atmosphere:** fine dust, drifting particles, distant movement that suggests a living world.
- **Golden light travels through the architecture like veins beneath dark stone.**

## The curriculum
| Subject | Visual |
|---|---|
| Mathematics - Architecture of Gravity | A stone sphere rises, rotates, orbits a geometric structure; equations are part of the space |
| Science - Birth of Galaxies | Particles to luminous core to spiral arms in a containment field |
| History - The Living Past | An inhabited historical place; the students are physically present |
| Art - Imagination Animated | A drawing gains dimension, lifts off the page, becomes alive |
| Final Examination | Survive a world created by your own imagination |

Wrong-note details the couple notices: a door that moves when unseen; a painting of a ruined tower visible through a window; portraits covered with dark cloth.

## Symbols
- **Seal symbol:** appears in the fall fissures, on the sealed door and beneath the faceless mural figures.
- **Bond mark:** two interwoven lines continuing into each other with no beginning or end. Not a chain, not a lock.
- **Gold kintsugi cracks:** the signature of the sphere/being.

## The mystery
The academy was built around a sphere that reflects nothing. A being once radiant broke into golden fissures; the sphere remained. Someone destroyed the last mural, except two joined-hand figures with missing faces. The sphere answers to a bonded pair. Something behind it has noticed them.

# Cinematography

Format: 9:16 vertical (1080x1920). Photoreal cinematic fantasy. Frame for a phone screen.

## Principles
- Every camera move serves the story. No variety for its own sake.
- Slow, controlled, physically possible moves. No handheld shake, no fantasy filters.
- Isolate complex actions: one shot = at most one character pair + one big transformation + one camera move.
- 3-layer depth: foreground / middle / background, always separated.
- Vertical advantage: show floor detail low in frame and tall architecture high in frame.

## Shot types
| Shot | Purpose | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Extreme close-up | small anomalies | pencil, cracks, eye, hands |
| Close-up | emotion | uncertainty, realization |
| Medium | interaction | Lucien and Elara near the desk |
| Wide | location | classroom, portal |
| Extreme wide | scale | academy, clouds |
| Low angle | monumental | towers |
| Slow push-in | anticipation | the dark patch |
| Tracking | progression | entering the academy |
| Crane rise | reveal | academy interior |

## Light and colour
- Classroom: restrained, cool dusk.
- Portal: gold enters gradually.
- Academy: cool shadow + 2700K warm gold.
- Chamber: darker, less warm, almost no source.
- Grade progression, not one filter for everything.

## Prompt layers (reuse across shots)
A. visual identity - B. character identity - C. composition - D. one primary action - E. physical behaviour - F. cinematic treatment - G. constraints (what must not change).

Default negatives: cartoon rendering, plastic skin, excess bloom, random glowing symbols, camera shake, inconsistent faces, extra limbs, distorted hands, sudden costume changes, unmotivated cuts.

## ACT I - THE WORLD THAT NOTICES THEM (0:00-1:30)

### Scene 1 - An ordinary evening
An almost empty school classroom. Evening light falls through the windows. Chairs are slightly misaligned; the last students have left.

Lucien and Elara are still there.

We establish their relationship through small, believable details. She takes his pen without asking. He moves her bag before it falls. They exchange a look that suggests familiarity rather than a dramatic, newly discovered attraction.

They don't need to announce that they're in love. We should feel it.

Then something strange happens.

A pencil rolls across a desk - but stops at the edge, pointing toward an empty patch of floor.

The shadows beneath the desks begin stretching in the wrong direction.

Elara notices first. Lucien follows her gaze.

### Scene 2 - The impossible crack
The darkness beneath a desk becomes deeper than the space should allow.

A thin golden fissure appears within it.

The crack spreads across the floor, but it doesn't break the tiles. It seems to split the space above them, revealing darkness behind reality itself.

The classroom lights flicker.

Elara reaches for Lucien's hand. He takes it.

The fissures race toward the centre of the room, joining into a single shape.

For one instant, everything stops.

Then the floor disappears.

### Scene 3 - The fall
They fall together.

Not into an ordinary tunnel, but through fragments of impossible worlds: a forest growing upside down, a distant ocean suspended in the sky, enormous stone arches floating through darkness.

Their hands slip apart. Lucien catches Elara's wrist. She grabs his hand again. They continue falling.

Neither knows where they're going. Neither lets go.

*Emotional purpose:* their first major choice is already established. In a world that has become incomprehensible, they reach for each other.

---

## ACT II - THE ACADEMY BEYOND REALITY (1:30-3:30)

### Scene 4 - The first sight
They land on a vast black-stone causeway. For several seconds, neither speaks.

Lucien gets up first and helps Elara stand. She checks that he's okay before looking around.

Then the camera rises.

An immense academy hangs above an endless sea of clouds. Towers disappear into the mist. Bridges connect structures that shouldn't be able to remain suspended. A staircase slowly rearranges itself in the distance.

Golden lights travel through the architecture like veins beneath dark stone.

For the first time, the film's music opens into something enormous.

Elara takes a step forward. Lucien watches her expression, then looks back at the academy.

It is beautiful. But something about it feels wrong.

### Scene 5 - The school that shouldn't exist
They enter through monumental doors.

Inside, the academy is alive with impossible activity. A student writes a mathematical equation in the air, and the objects around them rotate as gravity changes. A glass sphere contains a miniature galaxy. Somewhere in a distant hall, an illustrated creature moves inside a book.

But the important part isn't simply showing magical things. The couple begins noticing details that don't fit:
- A doorway changes position when they look away.
- A painting depicts a ruined tower that they can see through a distant window.
- A student briefly notices their joined hands and becomes visibly uneasy.

Nobody explains anything.

Eventually, they reach a corridor where every portrait has been covered with dark cloth. At its far end is a door bearing an unfamiliar symbol.

The same symbol appeared in the golden fissures during their fall.

Elara recognises it. Lucien realises she has stopped walking. They approach the door.

*Purpose:* the academy has a history, rules and secrets that the protagonists have accidentally entered.

---

## ACT III - THE THING THAT WAS NEVER FINISHED (3:30-5:30)

### Scene 6 - The unfinished creature
Behind a partly open door, they discover a forgotten classroom. Its tables are covered with old parchment. Sketches of strange creatures cover the walls. Most are crossed out or burned.

One drawing is different. It depicts a creature with an elongated body, feathered wings, and a featureless face. The drawing ends abruptly at the creature's chest, as if its creator stopped halfway through.

Elara approaches it. One ink line moves. She freezes.

The creature's head slowly rises from the page. Its eye opens. Its unfinished wing emerges, casting a shadow across the desk.

It is alive. But it shouldn't be.

Lucien pulls Elara back as the creature slips off the parchment and lands on the floor.

It doesn't attack them. Instead, it stares at their joined hands.

A thin golden line appears across its body. The creature turns toward the sealed corridor. Then it begins walking. They follow.

### Scene 7 - The academy's missing history
The creature leads them into an abandoned section of the academy. The polished stone becomes cracked. The golden lights grow dimmer.

They discover murals showing the academy at different points in its history.

- One mural depicts a beautiful, radiant being surrounded by people.
- Another depicts the same being breaking apart into golden fissures.
- In the final mural, the academy's towers surround a dark sphere. The being is gone. The sphere remains.

Someone has deliberately destroyed the mural's final section.

Lucien examines the damaged stone. Elara notices a detail the destruction failed to erase: two figures standing before the sphere, their hands joined. Their faces are missing. Beneath them is a symbol matching the one on the sealed door.

Before they can investigate further, the creature suddenly recoils. The golden line on its body spreads. For a moment, something moves beneath its skin - something larger than the creature itself. Then the line disappears.

The creature collapses into ink. The ink flows across the floor toward the sealed door.

Silence.

Lucien and Elara understand that whatever they've discovered is connected to something dangerous.

---

## ACT IV - THE CHAMBER THAT REMEMBERS (5:30-7:30)

### Scene 8 - The forbidden room
The sealed door opens.

Inside is a circular chamber surrounded by enormous black-stone pillars. Metal rings rotate around a black-glass sphere suspended above the floor.

Unlike the rest of the academy, the chamber has no visible source of light.

The sphere reflects nothing. Not the pillars. Not the ceiling. Not even Lucien and Elara.

As they enter, the metal rings begin slowing down. One by one, they stop. The entire academy seems to fall silent.

Then the sphere changes.

### Scene 9 - The memory
Golden fissures spread across its surface. At first, they form a chaotic network. Then the lines begin rearranging themselves.

The pattern becomes recognisable: two hands, interlaced. Exactly like Lucien and Elara's hands.

They look down at their fingers. Then back at the sphere.

Elara slowly tries to separate her hand from Lucien's. The golden pattern begins breaking apart. She stops. She looks at him.

He doesn't tell her to hold on. He doesn't promise that everything will be fine. He simply turns his hand slightly and lets her choose.

She intertwines her fingers with his again. The fissures reconnect.

This time, something else happens. The sphere produces an image - not a reflection of their bodies, but two silhouettes standing together beneath a sky filled with unfamiliar stars.

The silhouettes are Lucien and Elara. They are older in one image, younger in another. Their clothes change. The architecture around them changes. One image dissolves into the next. But in every image, they find each other.

The sphere is showing them something that resembles a memory of lives they have never lived.

Neither can explain it. Neither can look away.

### Scene 10 - The realisation
Elara approaches the sphere.

For a moment, she sees another version of herself standing on the other side of the glass. That version reaches toward a figure beside her. Lucien.

The image fractures.

A faint golden line appears across Elara's palm. The same line appears on Lucien's.

They look at their hands. Then at each other.

Their expressions change - not because a magical object has told them whom to love, but because they are confronting something neither of them can explain.

They have chosen each other in this life. Yet the sphere seems to suggest that their connection reaches beyond this single moment, perhaps beyond this single lifetime.

But they don't yet know whether the sphere is showing them a truth, a possibility, or a trap.

That uncertainty matters. If the film simply announces that they're destined to be together, their love becomes a prophecy they must obey. If it reveals that something has recognised a bond they already chose, their love remains theirs.

---

## ACT V - THE ETERNAL MARK (7:30-9:00)

### Scene 11 - The choice
The sphere begins to crack. Not physically - the golden lines are spreading across the darkness behind its surface.

The images disappear.

For the first time, the couple sees something behind the sphere: an enormous shape, barely distinguishable from the surrounding void.

The academy was not merely hiding the sphere. It was containing something.

A deep vibration passes through the chamber. The metal rings begin rotating in the opposite direction.

The golden mark on Lucien's palm brightens. Elara's mark answers it.

The sphere shows them one final image. Two figures stand on opposite sides of a great golden fracture. Between them is the same darkness they saw beneath the classroom desk. One figure reaches for the other. The image ends before their hands meet.

The chamber begins collapsing. Lucien pulls Elara toward the exit, but the door seals itself. A golden fissure races between them across the floor.

The sphere's light intensifies. For an instant, the two marks pull in opposite directions - as if something is trying to separate them.

Elara looks at Lucien. They have no proof that staying together will save them. They have no idea whether the sphere is showing them a warning or attempting to manipulate them.

But they understand one thing: whatever this place wants from them, they will face it together.

Lucien extends his hand. Elara takes it.

Not because the sphere commands them. Because she chooses him. Because he chooses her.

The marks align. A single golden line connects their palms. The chamber stops shaking. The darkness behind the sphere withdraws.

But it does not disappear. It has noticed them.

### Scene 12 - The mark of an eternal bond
The sphere becomes still. Its surface clears for the first time, revealing two small points of golden light suspended in the darkness. They orbit one another. They never collide. They never drift apart.

As the lights move, the golden marks on Lucien and Elara's palms form a complete symbol when their hands are joined.

The symbol is not a chain. It is not a lock. It resembles two interwoven lines that continue into one another without a visible beginning or end.

Elara traces the mark with her thumb. She looks at Lucien, and he understands what she is asking without her speaking. He turns his palm toward hers. The marks meet.

For a brief moment, their reflections appear on the sphere - not as they are now, but as two figures standing together beneath a night sky filled with stars. The vision fades.

They remain where they are, holding hands.

They have not been given a complete explanation. They do not know what the mark will demand from them, whether their bond truly transcends lifetimes, or why the academy has been waiting for them. But they now have reason to believe that their connection is part of something much older than either of them.

*That is the emotional centre of the film.* The mark is evidence of the mystery. Their decision to remain together is evidence of their love. Those are related, but they are not the same thing.

---

## ACT VI - THE THING THAT WOKE UP (9:00-10:00)

### Scene 13 - The academy changes
The couple leaves the chamber. The corridor looks different. Golden light is travelling through the walls, following the same pattern as the marks on their palms.

Far above them, the academy's enormous dome stops rotating. Every clock in the building stops at the same instant. Students emerge from classrooms. Teachers look toward the restricted wing.

Lucien and Elara reach a balcony overlooking the clouds. The academy stretches before them, beautiful and immense.

Then every window in the distant tower turns black. One by one.

A low sound rises from somewhere beneath the structure. The clouds below the academy begin rotating around an enormous empty space.

For a fraction of a second, the couple sees something moving in that darkness. The shape from behind the sphere. The thing the academy has been containing.

It has not escaped. Not yet.

But the golden mark on their palms pulses once. Far beneath them, something answers.

Elara looks at Lucien. He looks at their joined hands. Neither speaks.

The camera pulls back, revealing the academy suspended above the darkness.

Cut to black.

# THE UNWRITTEN

*Final implication:* their arrival did not simply reveal the academy to them. It changed the balance of something the academy has been trying to keep hidden. And the connection between them may be the only thing capable of changing what happens next.

That is the emotional centre of the film.

The mark is evidence of the mystery.

Their decision to remain together is evidence of their love.

Those are related, but they are not the same thing.

🎬 THE UNWRITTEN — PRODUCTION MASTER PLAN V2
Project: THE UNWRITTEN
Universe: FANTASY-LAND
Genre: Cinematic fantasy, mystery, coming-of-age
Initial development target: 85–90 seconds
Competition deliverable: 45–60 seconds
Long-term target: Approximately 10-minute short film
Visual ambition: Photorealistic environments, convincing physical interactions, sophisticated magical effects, coherent character performances, and emotionally motivated cinematography.
PHASE 0 — THE WINNING STRATEGY
We need to stop thinking of this as one long AI generation.

We're producing a sequence of individually controlled shots that must eventually behave like a coherent film.

Our production pipeline will be:
1. Creative brief and competition requirements.
2. Original story and narrative structure.
3. Worldbuilding and mythology.
4. Protagonist design and character consistency.
5. Visual development and reference images.
6. Cinematography and shot planning.
7. Detailed generation prompts.
8. Image and video generation.
9. Dialogue, narration, music, and sound design.
10. Editing, compositing, and continuity correction.
11. Technical quality assurance.
12. Competition submission.
13. Expansion into the 10-minute short film.
Every phase produces something concrete that we can inspect before proceeding.

No generating 40 random fantasy clips and hoping they become a movie.
PHASE 1 — CREATIVE BRIEF
1.1 The central premise
An ordinary school conceals an entrance to an impossible academy.

There, students study disciplines unknown to the ordinary world: manipulating gravity through mathematics, creating miniature galaxies through science, entering forgotten civilizations through history, and bringing imagined creatures into existence through art.

Two students discover the academy.

They believe they've stumbled upon something extraordinary.

But the academy has been expecting them.

An ancient prophecy identifies two individuals whose abilities could fundamentally alter reality. Their arrival coincides with the weakening of a seal protecting the school from a creation that should never have existed.

The students must discover why they were chosen before the academy's greatest secret escapes.
1.2 The audience experience
We want the film to deliver five escalating experiences.
Stage	Intended response	Production technique
Intrigue	Something is wrong.	Anomalous shadows, silence, restrained movement
Suspense	What is beneath that desk?	Gradual visual escalation, close-ups, sound design
Wonder	This world is impossible.	Monumental establishing shots, layered environments
Fascination	What do they teach here?	Distinctive magical disciplines and transformations
Unease	Why were these two chosen?	Prophecy, contrasting powers, unexplained consequences


The film should not reveal everything.

The academy is the immediate spectacle. The prophecy is the narrative hook. The hidden danger is what makes the audience want the next chapter.
1.3 Originality requirements
We can learn from successful fantasy films without copying their protected expression.

Our academy needs its own:
- Architectural identity.
- Magical principles.
- Educational system.
- Historical mythology.
- Visual symbols.
- Character abilities.
- Prophecy.
- Central conflict.
- Sound motifs.
Gothic architecture and golden magic are broad visual ingredients. The specific designs, scenes, dialogue, characters, and mythology we create must be our own.
PHASE 2 — THE WORLD BIBLE
This is where we move beyond pretty scenery.
2.1 The ordinary world
The opening location should be believable.

A recognizable school classroom, ordinary furniture, fluorescent lighting, bags on desks, a corridor outside, and the subdued ambience of a building after the final bell.

Why?

Because the more convincing the ordinary world feels, the more extraordinary the transition becomes.

We should photograph or recreate a consistent classroom environment rather than asking each generation to invent an entirely different one.

Continuity rule: The classroom's desk positions, windows, lighting direction, and portal location must remain consistent throughout the opening sequence.
2.2 The portal
The portal is not a conventional blue or purple science-fiction ring.

Its visual identity is darkness that appears to absorb surrounding light.

The sequence progresses through six states:
1. Shadows stretch toward a fixed point.
2. Small objects move against gravity.
3. The darkest region loses visible surface detail.
4. Thin golden fissures appear within the darkness.
5. The fissures spread as the opening expands.
6. A physically convincing view into another world becomes visible.
Each state should have a distinct visual purpose.

We should not ask a video model to generate all six states perfectly in one long clip. We can generate the most difficult transformation as separate shots and control the transitions during editing.
2.3 The academy
The academy should have a consistent architectural language.

Primary materials
- Weathered dark stone.
- Aged bronze and blackened metal.
- Tall, intricately carved wooden doors.
- Teal-tinted stained glass.
- Reflective black surfaces.
- Warm golden magical illumination.
Architectural motifs
- Monumental Gothic arches.
- Impossible vertical spaces.
- Bridges suspended over clouds.
- Staircases that move independently.
- Libraries extending beyond visible boundaries.
- Vast astronomical chambers.
Lighting
- Cool ambient illumination in shadowed spaces.
- Warm gold around magical phenomena.
- Volumetric light through windows.
- Realistic reflections and shadow falloff.
- Controlled highlights rather than indiscriminate glowing effects.
Atmosphere
- Fine suspended dust.
- Occasional drifting particles.
- Distant movement that suggests a living world.
- Deep foreground, middle-ground, and background separation.
We should reserve the largest visual effects for key moments. If every object glows, the portal and prophecy lose their visual importance.
2.4 The magical curriculum
Every subject must introduce a different kind of visual spectacle.

Mathematics — The Architecture of Gravity

Students manipulate spatial equations that alter the gravitational field around physical objects.

The visual demonstration: a stone sphere rises, rotates, and begins orbiting a geometric structure.

The equations must feel like part of the scene, not random decorative symbols.

Science — The Birth of Galaxies

A miniature galaxy forms inside a transparent containment field.

The visual sequence moves from particles to a luminous core, then to spiral arms and tiny stars.

The effect should communicate controlled creation rather than an ordinary explosion.

History — The Living Past

Students enter an accurately designed historical environment that feels inhabited.

The past should not appear as a holographic projection. The students must seem physically present inside the environment, with matching light, shadows, scale, and perspective.

Art — The Animation of Imagination

A drawn creature gains dimensionality, separates from its canvas, and becomes a living entity.

The transformation needs intermediate stages so that it feels like a physical process rather than an arbitrary cut.

The Final Examination — The Uncontrolled Creation

Students must create a new world, but the world begins to develop beyond their intentions.

The danger is not simply that the creature they create is powerful.

It is that their creation begins rewriting the rules they depend on.
PHASE 3 — CHARACTER DESIGN AND CONSISTENCY
This is one of the biggest technical risks in AI filmmaking.

If the protagonists look different every time the camera angle changes, the film will feel artificial.

We need a character reference package for each protagonist.
3.1 Character reference package
For each character, establish:
1. Front-facing portrait.
2. Three-quarter portrait.
3. Side profile.
4. Full-body reference.
5. Neutral standing pose.
6. Expression sheet.
7. Clothing reference.
8. Color palette.
9. Relative height and proportions.
10. Lighting-neutral reference.
We should also create a two-character reference showing their relative heights and appearances.

These references are not ten separate shots that must appear in the film. They are assets for controlling generation.
3.2 Clothing design
The academy's uniforms should have an original silhouette.

My initial direction:
- Long, tailored charcoal coats.
- Subtle dark-teal lining.
- Minimal bronze fasteners.
- An original geometric academy emblem.
- No oversized decorative accessories.
- Natural fabric folds and movement.
- Distinctive details that remain visible in medium shots.
The two protagonists should share the uniform system but have recognizable differences.

For example, one might have a more structured silhouette and the other a more relaxed one.

We'll decide the final details after establishing the protagonists' appearances.
3.3 Character performance
Photorealism isn't just about faces.

It also depends on behavior.

Characters should:
- Look toward the object that attracts their attention.
- React to changes in light and sound.
- Shift their weight naturally.
- Maintain plausible personal space.
- Avoid unnecessary hand gestures.
- Move at a speed consistent with their emotional state.
- Pause when something genuinely overwhelms them.
For the academy reveal, the most effective performance may be nearly motionless.

The world moves around them.

They simply stare.

That contrast communicates scale.
PHASE 4 — THE CINEMATOGRAPHY BIBLE
We need to decide how the film is photographed before generating individual shots.
4.1 Camera language
Shot type	Purpose	Application
Extreme close-up	Reveal small anomalies	Shadows, cracks, eyes
Close-up	Capture emotion	Fear, uncertainty, realization
Medium shot	Establish interaction	The protagonists approaching the desk
Wide shot	Establish location	Classroom and portal
Extreme wide shot	Communicate scale	Academy, floating islands
Low-angle shot	Emphasize monumental structures	Academy towers
Slow push-in	Build anticipation	The dark portal
Tracking shot	Create physical progression	Entering the academy
Controlled crane or rising movement	Reveal architecture	The academy's enormous interior


We should not use every shot type simply to show technical variety.

Every camera movement must support the story.
4.2 Lens and depth
We should describe the intended image rather than relying entirely on lens terminology, because different generation tools interpret camera specifications inconsistently.

For example:
Wide cinematic composition, foreground desk sharply defined, protagonists in the middle ground, monumental Gothic architecture visible beyond the portal, deep atmospheric perspective, realistic optical depth, controlled highlights, physically plausible shadows.

This communicates a desired result without depending on the model correctly simulating a particular lens.
4.3 Motion constraints
Video generation often struggles when a prompt simultaneously demands:
- Two consistent characters.
- Complex body movement.
- A changing environment.
- A transforming portal.
- Flying objects.
- Moving camera.
- Dramatic lighting changes.
We should isolate complex actions.

A shot might contain a stationary camera, two characters, and one portal transformation.

The next shot can reveal the world beyond it.

This makes the production more controllable.
PHASE 5 — THE SHOT-BY-SHOT PRODUCTION PLAN
For the initial 85–90-second version, we should aim for approximately 15–20 shots, depending on the pacing and capabilities of the available tools.

That does not mean every shot requires a separate text-to-video generation.

Some shots can be produced through image animation, controlled camera movement, compositing, or a combination of techniques.

Each shot will have its own production record.

Required shot record:
- Shot ID.
- Narrative purpose.
- Duration.
- Character identities.
- Location.
- Starting composition.
- Camera movement.
- Subject movement.
- Environmental movement.
- Lighting.
- Sound cues.
- Generation method.
- Reference assets.
- Prompt.
- Negative constraints where supported.
- Continuity requirements.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Fallback plan.
This is the level of detail we need.
Example: Shot 01 — The Last Classroom
Narrative purpose: Establish the ordinary world before introducing the anomaly.

Duration: Approximately 4 seconds.

Composition: A medium-wide view of a classroom at dusk, with the two protagonists near the same desk.

Camera: Very slow forward movement, almost imperceptible.

Lighting: Cool ambient daylight from the windows, ordinary classroom lighting, soft realistic shadows.

Character behavior: The students are packing their belongings. Their movements are natural and understated.

Environmental motion: Slight movement in clothing and loose paper, with no supernatural effects yet.

Sound: Distant corridor ambience, faint electrical hum, a chair moving across the floor.

Acceptance criteria:
- Both characters are visually consistent with their approved references.
- The desk and classroom layout are clearly readable.
- No unintended magical effects appear.
- Lighting and shadows are physically plausible.
- No obvious facial, hand, or object distortions.
- The framing supports a vertical 9:16 crop.
Fallback: If character movement is unreliable, use a carefully composed reference image with restrained camera movement, then create the next shot separately.

That is the difference between writing a prompt and planning a production.
PHASE 6 — PROMPT ENGINEERING
We should use a layered prompt system.

Instead of writing a completely new prompt from scratch for every shot, we'll define reusable components.
Layer A — Visual identity
Establish the academy's architecture, materials, lighting, and atmosphere.
Layer B — Character identity
Reference the approved protagonists and specify their clothing, appearance, and positions.
Layer C — Shot composition
Specify framing, subject placement, depth, and camera behavior.
Layer D — Action
Describe one primary action and its progression.
Layer E — Physical behavior
Describe shadows, reflections, cloth movement, particles, and object interactions.
Layer F — Cinematic treatment
Specify the intended realism, color response, contrast, and atmospheric depth.
Layer G — Constraints
Specify what must not change: faces, clothing, architecture, object count, portal location, or camera position.

Not every tool supports every control, so we will adapt the prompts to the actual generator rather than assuming a universal syntax.
Example: The Portal Emerges
Prompt draft — image reference or video-generation model
Create an original photorealistic fantasy-film shot inside a believable school classroom at dusk. Two teenage student protagonists stand beside a wooden desk, their appearance and clothing matching the supplied character references exactly. Beneath the desk, a small region of darkness begins absorbing the surrounding light. Nearby shadows gradually stretch toward it. Loose paper and dust lift gently from the floor as the anomaly intensifies. Thin, intricate golden fissures emerge within the darkness, illuminating the desk's underside and the students' faces with subtle, physically plausible reflected light.

Maintain the same classroom layout, desk position, character identities, clothing, body proportions, and lighting direction throughout the shot. Use a controlled, slow camera push toward the anomaly. The characters remain cautious and physically grounded, reacting with restrained surprise rather than exaggerated gestures. Preserve realistic materials, detailed skin texture, natural fabric folds, believable object movement, and consistent spatial perspective.

The atmosphere should feel mysterious and monumental without becoming visually cluttered. Use deep teal shadows and restrained golden illumination. The darkness itself should remain the dominant visual element.

Avoid cartoon rendering, illustrated surfaces, plastic-looking skin, excessive bloom, random glowing symbols, uncontrolled camera shake, inconsistent faces, duplicated limbs, distorted hands, floating objects without physical cause, sudden costume changes, and unmotivated scene transitions.

Important: This is a draft for the first visual test, not a guarantee that any particular model will render it correctly. We'll adapt the prompt to the selected generator and inspect the output.

If the model cannot create the complete transformation consistently, we'll split the shot into a starting frame, an intermediate state, and the final portal state.
PHASE 7 — AUDIO AND SOUND DESIGN
Sound should be developed alongside the visuals, not added as an afterthought.

We need five layers.

1. Environmental ambience

The classroom, corridor, distant bells, and subtle room tone establish physical reality.

2. Low-frequency tension

A restrained drone introduces the anomaly. It grows gradually rather than immediately becoming loud.

3. Magical sound identity

The portal receives a distinctive sound built from layered textures, reversed elements, and a low resonant component.

4. Musical development

The academy reveal introduces the main musical theme. The prophecy interrupts or transforms it.

5. Silence

The final reveal may work better if the sound drops away immediately before the final line.

Silence creates contrast.

For narration, we should use a consistent voice, natural pacing, and clear diction. We should avoid stacking multiple dramatic voices over every scene.

For music, we can create an original cinematic cue with a defined progression:
- Restrained opening.
- Rising tension.
- Expansive academy reveal.
- Brief musical wonder during the curriculum.
- Dark tonal shift during the prophecy.
- Sudden silence at the final reveal.
We should also keep the narration intelligible over the music and avoid clipping.
PHASE 8 — EDITING AND COMPOSITING
The editing process will determine whether the shots feel like one film.
8.1 Build the timeline first
Create the sequence at the intended vertical aspect ratio.

Place temporary shots or reference frames into the timeline according to the approved script.

Establish pacing before spending time polishing every individual clip.
8.2 Match the transitions
The portal sequence should have visual continuity between shots.

For example:
- A golden fissure appears beneath the desk.
- A close-up reveals the fissure spreading.
- A wider shot shows the portal opening.
- A reverse angle reveals the academy beyond it.
The viewer should understand that these are different views of the same event.
8.3 Color grading
The ordinary classroom begins with relatively restrained colors.

As the portal develops, golden highlights enter the image.

The academy uses a broader range of cool shadows and warm illumination.

The prophecy sequence introduces darker tones and reduces the warmth.

The goal is not to apply the same filter to everything. It is to establish a coherent visual progression.
8.4 Compositing
Where necessary, we can combine separately generated elements:
- Background environment.
- Character footage or animation.
- Magical particles.
- Portal light.
- Foreground objects.
- Additional shadows and highlights.
Compositing is useful when a generator cannot maintain all the required elements simultaneously.

However, every composite needs matching perspective, lighting, grain, and movement.

Otherwise, the magic looks pasted on.
PHASE 9 — QUALITY ASSURANCE
Before accepting a shot, inspect it against a checklist.

# ACT I — SOMETHING IS WRONG

## SCENE 01 — AN ORDINARY EVENING
**Time: 00:00–00:05**

Black screen.

A faint electrical hum.

A chair scrapes against the floor.

Cut to a realistic school classroom at dusk. Wooden desks occupy the foreground; empty chairs and schoolbags sit in the middle distance. Tall windows reveal a dimming sky. The room is ordinary, quiet, and still recognisably part of the real world.

Only two students remain: a boy and a girl who are in love.

The boy is collecting his belongings while the girl checks the contents of her bag. As he reaches for a notebook, it slips from the edge of the desk. She catches it before it falls and hands it back to him. Their fingers briefly touch. They exchange a small, familiar smile—private and unperformed, as if this kind of tiny moment is normal between them.

A ceiling light flickers once.

Neither reacts.

The camera begins a very slow forward movement. Somewhere outside, faint footsteps echo down the corridor.

The girl moves a notebook aside to reach a pencil. The pencil rolls a short distance across the desk.

Everything still appears normal.

**Camera:** A controlled, nearly imperceptible push forward. No dramatic shake or exaggerated cinematic effects. The viewer should feel physically present in the classroom.

**Sound:** Low classroom room tone, faint distant footsteps, fabric and bag movement, pencil rolling against wood, and a quiet electrical hum. No dramatic music yet.

The affection is visible, but understated. The scene must not stop to explain their relationship.

Nothing extraordinary has happened.

That is intentional.

## SCENE 02 — SOMETHING STOPS
**Time: 00:05–00:09**

Close-up of the pencil near the edge of the desk.

It stops rolling—not because it hits anything. It simply stops.

The camera holds.

A loose sheet of paper beside it begins moving slowly toward the desk's edge. Then it changes direction and slides toward the floor.

The camera tilts downward to follow it.

Underneath the desk is a patch of darkness. At first, it looks like an ordinary shadow. But it is too deep. The floor should be visible behind the desk legs; instead, a small section of the floor appears to have disappeared.

The surrounding shadows begin stretching toward that point.

The desk legs remain stationary. The paper slides over the floor with believable friction. Dust moves toward the darkness. The room's lighting remains almost unchanged everywhere else.

This is not an explosion of magic. It is a local violation of reality.

The boy notices the paper moving and looks down. The girl follows his gaze. They exchange a quick, uncertain look: neither wants to overreact, but both know they saw the same thing.

**Camera:** Move from the pencil to the paper, then follow it downward. End with the dark region clearly visible beneath the desk.

**Sound:** The pencil's rolling sound stops abruptly. The electrical hum loses some of its higher frequencies. A faint low-frequency rumble begins. The paper scrapes against the floor, followed by a subtle sound resembling distant wind beneath the desk.

The shadow is not behaving normally.

## SCENE 03 — THE ROOM PULLS AWAY
**Time: 00:09–00:14**

Low-angle shot from near floor level.

The dark patch spreads beneath the desk. Its boundary advances across the floor, but the surrounding floor does not crack or physically break. Reflected light simply disappears wherever the darkness passes. A narrow reflection from the window vanishes, and the desk's underside becomes harder to distinguish.

The boy and girl exchange another look. Their concern is quiet and instinctive. The boy shifts closer to her; she briefly catches his sleeve, not to make a dramatic declaration, but because the room suddenly feels unsafe.

They move away from the desk together, retreating toward the front of the classroom. Keep their movement grounded and continuous. By the end of this scene, both are standing near the front of the room, looking back toward the desk at the rear.

A piece of paper lifts from the floor, rises several centimetres, rotates slowly, and drifts toward the dark region. It does not fly around like it is caught in a tornado. Its movement is controlled and unnerving.

**Camera:** A slow lateral movement reveals the darkness spreading from beneath the desk. Remain low long enough to make the anomaly feel larger than it actually is, then let the boy and girl' retreat establish their distance from it.

**Sound:** A soft, irregular vibration; paper fluttering; a faint metallic resonance; and a subtle rise in tension. No giant magical impact.

Something is pulling the environment toward it.

## SCENE 04 — THE FIRST GOLDEN FISSURE
**Time: 00:14–00:19**

The boy and girl remain at the front of the classroom, with their backs and shoulders partly visible in the foreground. The desk and the dark anomaly are at the far end of the room.

Extreme close-up of the darkness beneath the desk.

For a moment, absolutely nothing is visible inside it.

Then a very thin golden line appears.

The boy and girl do not notice it yet. Their attention is uncertainly fixed on the room and on each other; the fissure is small and far away.

The line looks like a crack in a surface, except there is no visible surface to crack. It branches into two smaller fissures. A second line appears.

The golden light is restrained and concentrated. It casts a narrow, warm reflection onto the underside of the desk, but does not illuminate the whole classroom or reach the boy and girl in any obvious way.

A tiny particle of gold floats upward and disappears into the darkness.

The darkness pulses—not like ordinary water, but like space itself is flexing. Concentric distortions travel through the boundary, bending the outlines of the desk legs and briefly warping the background behind them. The effect should feel three-dimensional and unnatural, as if the room's geometry is being pressed from the other side.

**Camera:** From behind the two students, begin a slow, controlled move between their shoulders toward the desk at the back of the classroom. Their silhouettes frame the distant anomaly. Push forward only slightly; do not pass through the opening. Give the audience time to study the tiny golden fissure.

**Sound:** A delicate, high-frequency metallic tone. A deeper resonance answers it, followed by a single pulse almost like a heartbeat.

Something is on the other side.

## SCENE 05 — THEY NOTICE
**Time: 00:19–00:24**

Continue along the same camera axis, with the boy and girl still near the front of the classroom and the desk at the back.

The golden fissure grows a little brighter. A sheet of paper near the desk lifts and slides toward it, but the movement is too subtle for the boy and girl to notice immediately.

Then the spatial distortion pulses again.

For a fraction of a second, the desk and the wall behind it appear to bend out of alignment. A thin golden reflection flickers across a nearby chair. The sound reaches the boy and girl as a low, resonant note—not a loud bang.

The girl notices the reflection first. She turns toward the desk. The boy follows her gaze, then looks briefly at her, checking that she saw it too. She tightens her hold on his hand. He does not pull away.

They begin moving cautiously toward the desk, staying close together. Neither crouches beside it yet, and neither approaches recklessly. Their faces show confusion and disbelief, with a growing awareness that they may need to get away.

From their point of view, the darkness beneath the desk appears far deeper than the physical space could possibly allow. The golden fissures seem to extend into a place much larger than the classroom.

No exaggerated jaw drops. No unnatural slow-motion head turns. Their bodies respond to what they can actually see.

If dialogue is retained, keep it quiet and brief:

**Girl:** “Tell me you're seeing that.”

**Boy:** “I'm trying not to.”

These lines are provisional. They are spoken by the characters—not narration.

The low rumble becomes more noticeable. The classroom ambience remains faintly audible, reminding the audience that this is still happening in an ordinary room.

The two are seeing the same impossible phenomenon. They are not imagining it independently.

**End Act I:** Hold on the two of them standing together, looking toward the impossible darkness. Their joined hands remain visible at the edge of the frame.


# ACT II — THE PORTAL OPENS

## SCENE 06 — REALITY BEGINS TO BREAK
**Time: 00:24–00:31**

### What You See

**Every golden line is being pulled toward one central point.**

The surrounding floor appears to bend inward. The desk's legs distort as though the space around them is being compressed. Objects slide toward the center, while the air above the opening ripples with unnatural spatial distortions.

The golden lines connect, forming an intricate web across the darkness.

Then the light begins flowing inward.

The entire web dims, leaving a deep, lightless center. For one fraction of a second, the room becomes completely still.

A final golden crack reaches the center.

**The portal opens.**

The darkness parts—not like a door swinging open, but like reality itself has been separated.

The boy instinctively moves in front of the girl as the pressure wave reaches them. She catches his hand and pulls him back beside her. Neither abandons the other.

### Camera

Begin with a slow backward movement, revealing the expanding anomaly beneath the desk.

Gradually move between the boy and girl, keeping their shoulders in the foreground while the opening grows in the distance.

As the cracks converge, push slowly toward the center. Let the camera capture the impossible bending of the room without excessive shaking or rapid cuts.

When the final crack connects, hold the frame still for a brief moment.

### Sound

The classroom ambience gradually becomes muffled.

As the golden lines converge, introduce a low metallic groan, as though an enormous structure is shifting somewhere beyond the visible world.

When the final crack connects, cut the sound almost completely.

A single deep impact follows.

The portal opens with a powerful, resonant sound that seems to come from the space around the viewer rather than from the desk itself.

**No narration. Let the image and sound carry the moment.**
