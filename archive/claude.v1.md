**ARYAN** and **MAYA**.

### What changed and why (read this before the script)
- **Act I/II (yours) — kept almost as-is.** The notebook catch, the held hands, the fall through impossible space cutting to black — all good, all kept. Only trimmed repeated sentences so it reads as action instead of a shot-list essay.
- **Act III/IV — this is where your new draft reverted to the old problems.** It still had 3 back-to-back curriculum scenes and generic "What the audience learns" narration. Cut to ONE discipline, no narration.
- **The ending is the real fix.** Old draft: a sphere, an eye, a whisper, cut to black — no consequence, no connection to the fact these two are *together*. New version: the chamber doesn't react to "two chosen ones" in the abstract — it reacts to the fact that their hands are joined. The prophecy isn't a riddle anymore, it's specific: **the fissure pattern that forms on the sphere is the exact shape of their interlaced fingers from Scene 01.** It's been watching them as a pair since before the portal even opened. That's unsettling in a way "two will enter" never was, and it only works *because* you made them a couple — so use it.
- **The cost lands on both of them, matched.** Not one kid with a scar — both of them get the same thin gold line, in the same spot, where their hands joined. It's a bond mark, not a wound. Ends the reel on an image that's visually distinctive, thematically tied to the romance, and sets up exactly what the 10-minute film is about: whatever this place is, it's bound them to it, together.

*(your scenes, kept — the fall is a strong beat, keep it whole)*

**S06 (0:24–0:31)** Fissures spread, converge, the portal tears open — not a door swinging, reality separating. Aryan moves in front of Maya on instinct; she pulls him back beside her instead. Neither protects alone.

**S07 (0:31–0:38)** The academy resolves beyond the opening — impossible scale, towers into mist, bridges over a void. Wind starts pulling loose objects toward it. **One VO line only, here, quiet, in-character — not a trailer voice:**

**S08 (0:38–0:45)** Floor tilts toward the portal, gives way entirely. They fall together through impossible space — fragments of architecture flashing past, gravity shifting. Hands slip once mid-fall, find each other again. That near-loss of grip is scarier than any scream. Cut to black before landing — the audience doesn't know where they've landed, or if they're still together.

## ACT V — THE MARK (0:57–1:15)
*(fully rewritten — this replaces the sphere/eye/whisper ending)*

**S11 — The Restricted Chamber (0:57–1:01)**
Blackened door, asymmetric emblem. Opens on a dark chamber: rotating metal rings around a black-glass sphere reflecting nothing. Academy ambience cuts out completely the moment the door opens — let that silence do the work, no music cue needed to tell the audience this is wrong.

**S12 — It Recognizes Them (1:01–1:06)**
A ring slows. Gold fissures spread across the sphere. Aryan and Maya are still holding hands — they haven't let go since the fall. The fissure pattern on the sphere's surface **resolves into the exact shape of their interlaced fingers.** Not a word, not an eye — their own joined hands, mirrored back at them in cracked gold light.
MAYA, quiet, unsettled in a way she wasn't before: "...that's us."
ARYAN looks down at their joined hands, then back at the sphere. He doesn't let go. That's the choice that matters here — he could, and doesn't.

**S13 — The Mark (1:06–1:11)**
Sharp pulse. Both flinch at once — not separately. When they look down, a single thin gold line has appeared across both their palms, in the exact place their hands were joined. It doesn't glow dramatically. It's just there now, like it was always going to happen. They look at each other — not scared of each other, scared of what it means that it picked *them specifically*, together.
MAYA: "...Aryan."
He doesn't have an answer. Neither does she. Hold on their joined, marked hands — not their faces — as the last image before cut to black.

**S14 — Title (1:11–1:15)**
Black. The same thin gold line draws itself into the title shape.

## Why this version is stronger
- **The romance isn't decoration anymore — it's the mechanism.** The entity reacts to their bond specifically, which makes the couple framing earn its place in the story instead of just being a relationship happening alongside a generic portal plot.
- **One curriculum scene instead of three** keeps momentum going into Act V instead of stalling right before the stakes.
- **The ending gives you a genuinely distinctive final image** — two marked, joined hands — easy to thumbnail, easy to remember, and sets up the 10-minute film's actual premise (they're bound to this place together, not just "chosen").
- **Total VO: one line.** Judges have seen a hundred AI reels narrated like a trailer. Letting the visuals and the two actors carry it reads as more confident, not less finished.
